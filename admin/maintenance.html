Closing the site, and the banner across the top of it.

The public site

Closing the site puts a “back soon” page in front of every public page. It does not stop anybody working: the admin and editor areas are never closed, and anybody signed in as staff keeps seeing the real site with a bar across the top saying it is shut to everyone else.

What closing the site does, and does not do

  • It is a curtain, not a lock. The pages stop being shown in the browser. The rows behind them stay exactly as readable through the API as they were an hour ago, because the policies that decide that have not changed and should not. Close the site to stop people reading a page that is half-rewritten — never to hide something that must not be seen.
  • It arrives a moment after the page does. The setting is fetched by the reader’s browser, so on a first visit the real page can flash up before the curtain covers it. A repeat visit within the same session covers first and checks after. Neither is instant, and neither is meant to be.
  • Staff are not shut out. Editors and admins see the site as normal, with a bar saying it is closed to everyone else. The admin and editor areas are never curtained at all — otherwise turning this on would lock you out of turning it off.
  • Nothing here pauses the database. Editors can still publish while the site is closed, and what they publish is what readers will find when it opens.
