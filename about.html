About MedCare

What we publish, how we check it, and who stands behind it.

MedCare is a free health information site for Myanmar. It explains common conditions in plain language, points you to hospitals and pharmacies near you, and keeps the numbers you need in an emergency one tap away.

The short version

We take the health guidance published by the World Health Organization and Myanmar's Ministry of Health and rewrite it so it can be read and acted on without medical training — in English and in Burmese, on a phone, for free. We sell nothing, and we run no advertising.

Why we built it

Reliable health information about Myanmar exists, but it is scattered — across ministry circulars, international fact sheets, and clinical guidance written for doctors rather than for the people who need it. Someone worried about a fever at ten at night should not have to read a surveillance report to find out when to go to hospital.

So we put the answers in one place, in the words people actually use, and made sure the site opens quickly on a modest phone and an unreliable connection.

What you will find here

Common diseases

Symptoms, causes, treatment, and prevention for the conditions seen most often in Myanmar.

Health articles

Everyday guides on sleep, diet, hydration, mental health, hygiene, and staying well.

Hospitals and pharmacies

Directories you can filter by township, with locations and contact details.

Emergency contacts

Ambulance, poison control, and 24-hour hotlines, ready to dial.

There is also a BMI calculator that runs entirely in your browser, and you can save any page you want to come back to.

How a page gets written

Every condition and article follows the same route before it appears on the site:

  • Start from the primary guidance. We work from WHO material and Ministry of Health publications, not from other people's summaries of them.
  • Rewrite for a reader, not a clinician. Short sentences, no untranslated jargon, and the practical question — what do I do now, and when do I go to hospital — answered near the top.
  • Review before publishing. A second member of the editorial team checks the medical claims against the cited sources, and a page goes live only once it is approved.
  • Cite everything. Each page lists its sources with links, so you can read the original for yourself.
  • Date it. Pages carry the month they were last reviewed, so you can see how current they are.

English and Burmese

Health information is only useful in a language you read comfortably. Our condition pages and articles are written in both English and Burmese, and your choice of language is remembered on your device so the site opens the way you left it. Translation is done by the team, and the Burmese text is reviewed against the English rather than swapped in word for word.

What MedCare is not

We are not your doctor

Nothing here is a diagnosis, a prescription, or a substitute for professional medical advice. We cannot see you, examine you, or know your history. Use MedCare to understand a condition and to decide whether to seek care — then see a qualified doctor. In an emergency, call 192 for an ambulance rather than reading a web page.

We also do not answer personal medical questions, and we do not recommend particular doctors, clinics, or medicines. The hospital and pharmacy directories are listings, not endorsements, and no one pays to appear in them.

Who builds it

MedCare is the work of a small team of students who researched, wrote, designed, and built the site together. It is not funded by advertising, sponsorship, or any pharmaceutical company, and it costs nothing to use.

Meet the editorial team

Tell us when we get it wrong

We would rather hear about a mistake than leave it standing. If a page is out of date, a link is broken, a phone number has changed, or something reads as though it were written for a textbook, use the report button on the page itself or write to us directly. Corrections to medical content are the ones we act on first.
