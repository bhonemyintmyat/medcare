Roles and permissions

What each role may do, and who holds it.

…

Granting the editor role is the most consequential thing on this site. An editor writes and publishes what the site says about illness — what a symptom means, when to go to hospital, which emergency number to call. Give it to somebody whose medical judgement you would follow yourself, not to somebody who needs to fix a typo.

Change somebody’s role

Find the account, pick the role, confirm by typing their name. The change takes effect on their next page load; until then the database refuses the work anyway, whatever their browser thinks it is allowed to draw.

What each role may do

Every row names the policy or grant that enforces it, so any single cell can be checked against the database rather than believed.

This table is written down, not read from the database. Nothing in the browser can list the RLS policies — that needs pg_policies, which no anon key may read. So this is a description maintained by hand in admin-api.js, and it goes stale the moment somebody edits a policy without editing it. When the two disagree, the database is right and this is a bug.
