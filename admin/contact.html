What a reader sees

These are the only two ways to reach the team that contact.html offers: one email address and up to four phone numbers. There is no contact form on that page and nothing is collected from a reader there, so what is typed below is the whole of it. Editors keep the same details from their own copy of this screen; whichever of you saves last is what readers see.

The mailbox a reader's message will arrive at. This screen can only check that it looks like an address; whether mail sent there reaches a person is something no form can know, so send a test message to it and wait for a reply before trusting a change. A contact page pointing at an inbox nobody opens is worse than one with no address at all.

Up to 4.
With no address and no number saved, the Contact us page keeps the details written into the file it was published with. Nobody lands on a blank page — but the details a reader is given are then whatever was last deployed, not what is on this screen.

What this screen will not do

  • It does not touch the emergency numbers 192, 191, 199 and the helplines on emergency-contacts.html are health content. They are corrected in the editor area, by the people qualified to be sure they are right.
  • It is not admin-only, and deliberately so An editor who notices the office number has changed can correct it here without finding an admin, exactly as they can correct a hospital's. The database policy behind that names this one key: maintenance mode, the site-wide notice and the legal pages stay yours alone — supabase_contact_editors.sql.
  • It does not add a contact form A form would mean receiving whatever a frightened person types about their own health into a database this site has no reason to hold it in. An address and a number are enough.
  • It is public the moment it is saved site_settings is world-readable, including to visitors who are not signed in — that is how the Contact us page reads it. Never put a personal mobile here that should not be on a public page.
